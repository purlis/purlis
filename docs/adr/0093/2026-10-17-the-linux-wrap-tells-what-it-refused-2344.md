# Amended 2026-10-17: purlis's own wrap on Linux tells what it refused (#2344)

This builds the record "A block is learned from the sandbox first" proposed and held back for a
wrapped chat, on Linux, within the ruling on whose records purlis may read: its own child
processes only. On Linux the sandbox of a chat purlis wraps (Codex, opencode) is purlis's own
process, the chat's parent (ADR 0067, amended 2026-10-16), so its refusals are purlis's own
record and need no log, right or entitlement.

- **The supervisor's refusal is a denial record.** Each call the supervisor refuses by the
  chat's profile is told as macOS's kernel line says one: the operation in Seatbelt's word, the
  exact target the walk arrived at, and the name of the process that asked. It is sorted by the
  one sorter every block takes (`sandboxblock::supervised`), so the block, what Allow would
  name and the Notice's ways on (Allow once, for this chat, always, where a grant applies) are
  those of the same refusal on macOS.
- **Told by the wrap, over the chat's hook socket**, with the chat's token, as a hook tells a
  block, read from the environment the app started the chat with before the wrap holds itself
  to the profile. The app's throttle holds it as it holds any block line.
- **Telling never changes an answer.** The chat is refused first; what is told goes to a thread
  of its own through a short queue the supervisor never waits on. A refusal nobody hears is a
  refusal all the same. The wrap waits a moment at most, once its chat has ended, for what is
  still being told.
- **No flood.** The same operation on the same target is told once a minute, and at most ten
  different ones a minute.
- **Only what a person could act on.** A process's own files in `/proc`, a call refused for
  anything but the profile, and a connection to an internet address are not told: the last is
  purlis's proxy's to tell, by host.
- **One refusal, one Notice.** On Linux the hook of a chat purlis wraps leaves its files, local
  sockets and services to the supervisor, as it leaves hosts to the proxy.
