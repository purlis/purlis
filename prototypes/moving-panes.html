What's proposed

  1. One grip everywhere. Hover a persona icon, in the list or a pane's head, and it turns into dots with no button shape. The cursor is an open hand, and closes while you drag. A pane's blank head also drags, as it does today. The "Place…" button goes.
  2. A view on its own has no grip on it. Settings alone in a tab moves by its tab. In a split it has a head like any pane. So the grip never sits on a view's content, as it did over "You".
  3. Panes make room while you drag. The other panes move to where they'd end up, and what you're moving shows in its place. Let go elsewhere, or press Esc, and everything goes back.
  4. Drop zones don't move. They stay on the layout from before the drag, so panes making room can't shift a zone under your pointer and start a flicker.
  5. Terminals keep their size until you drop. A small label shows the size each will take. Resizing a live terminal on every pointer move would be slow and scramble its text.
  6. A new chat never replaces anything. It fills an empty pane if the tab has one, and otherwise gets a new tab.
  7. + opens an empty tab. Hover it, or press Down on it, for the list: empty tab, new chat, new shell, and whatever else joins the action list. An empty pane offers the same choices.
  8. Hold over a tab to go there. During a drag, resting on another tab for a moment brings it to the front, so a pane can land in it.
  9. Letting go on the pane itself does nothing and warns of nothing. Only a real refusal is red, and it sits on the pane it means.

Questions for you

My recommendation comes first in each. The buttons above the window let you feel the first two.

Pressing a chat that isn't on screenOne pane: show it there, and the old chat keeps running in the list, so switching stays fast. Split: new tab, so your layout survives. The alternative is always a new tab, which fills the strip quickly.
Should the dots show on row hover, or only on the icon?Only on the icon, so a list you run the mouse down doesn't lose its persona marks. The whole row still drags.
A drop at the far edge of a split tabKeep today's rule, where it splits just the pane under the pointer. Spanning the whole tab's height is a later addition.
Which key opens what?⌘T an empty tab and ⌘N a new chat. Today's new tab shortcut opens a chat, so this changes habits.
Empty tabs when you leave themKeep them until closed, like a browser's. Closing one never asks.
